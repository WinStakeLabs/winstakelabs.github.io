// The Problem

Why does this matter?

In Ukraine, the exotic animal market often resembles a "grey zone." People buy wild cats - lynxes, caracals, servals or ocelots - for status or a pretty picture, expecting them to behave like domestic cats.

But a wild animal remains wild. Due to a lack of knowledge, improper nutrition and inadequate conditions, animals are injured, fall ill, and eventually become unwanted. Once on the street, they are doomed.

// Personal experience

My Vatra

I know this not from hearsay. In my home lives Vatra - my incredible serval girl. She is socialized, well-raised and is a full member of my family. But every day, looking into her eyes, I see that enormous difference between a domestic cat and a wild predator.

I know how to provide her with a dignified life, and I understand how much effort and time it takes. Vatra and my communication with other owners of such animals inspired me to give a chance to others - those who have been left without a home, who suffered from human negligence, or whose health was undermined by improper care.

// Implementation plan

Steps toward the rehabilitation center

10% of all profit from my project goes into the fund for the future Rehabilitation and Sanctuary Center for wild cats. Our steps:

1
Territory
We are currently working on the most difficult stage - purchasing land for the center in the Zaporizhzhia region.
2
Infrastructure
We are designing spacious outdoor enclosures that are as close as possible to the natural environment, as well as warm winter quarters for species from other climatic zones. We already have an agreement with a similar center in Poland that will help with the design and construction of the enclosures.
3
First residents
At the start, the center will be able to accommodate and provide professional care for 5–10 wild cats, depending on whether the animals were in pairs or alone.
